As Turkish users, we have broken the Google Play service in a way that will be very difficult to undo. So how did this happen?

![](/media/weebly/1391785264-e1bbdc9d66.png)

A normal, sensible person says they like something if they like it, and says they don't if they don't. But that is no longer how things work on Google Play.

If you have spent some time on Google Play recently, you have probably come across plenty of absurd comments in Turkish. Of course, we cannot expect in-depth, IMDb-style reviews from such a large system that is accessible to every kind of person. But beyond a certain point, some comments can become really irritating. How so?

Many of the low-rated reviews posted from Turkey do not actually express a negative opinion about the application. Confused? Let me explain. An algorithm Google Play developed for reviews places low-rated comments higher up, so that other users and developers can more easily learn about an application's shortcomings. This worked properly until our clever(!) nation discovered the algorithm. Once we noticed it, however, we began giving even applications we liked one star so that our comments would appear at the top.

![](/media/weebly/1391788276-bab726ba92.jpg)

The situation I described above creates imbalances in an application's ratings chart, as shown on the right. This is a major problem for small companies that are just getting started in the mobile application world. Why?

Think of it this way: you are a small, unknown developer who has written a new application. What is the easiest way to gain recognition and succeed? Having your applications receive good ratings and enter the charts near the top. Applications with high scores will then be seen and downloaded by more people. But how would you react if people who downloaded your application suddenly dragged its average score down by giving it low ratings just so their comments would be visible? Frustrating, isn't it? And there is nothing concrete you can do about it. In the end, the application you put so much work into fails to receive the attention it deserves and disappears because of a few users trying to satisfy their egos.

The problem is not limited to comments that give one star to applications they like. You also encounter other meaningless comments. All of these ruin Google Play's usability and drive small mobile software companies towards bankruptcy.

Yes, that is how we broke Google Play. Foreign developers who initially could not make sense of these negative reviews are also beginning to realise what is happening. It is only a matter of time before we become a worldwide laughingstock. We should not be surprised if developers soon start deciding, one by one, not to release applications in our country. We are all responsible for this.
