*After the presidential election, I wanted to examine how successful each candidate had been compared with the local elections of 30 March. Here is my election analysis in numbers.*

---

## Let's Remember 30 March

![](/media/weebly/1407700704-e64f41e694.png)

First, I would like to give a brief summary of the local election results.

As you will remember, there were closely fought contests in many provinces and districts during the local elections. In some places, the winner was decided by just a handful of votes. There were allegations of electoral fraud in many places, particularly Ankara, and objections were filed against the results in those areas.

The results of the local elections on 30 March were approximately as follows.

In the mayoral elections:

- AKP: 17.9 million
- CHP: 11 million
- MHP: 7.4 million
- BDP/HDP: 2.5 million

votes were cast.

In the municipal council elections:

- AKP: 17.8 million
- CHP: 10.9 million
- MHP: 7.4 million
- BDP/HDP: 2.5 million

votes were cast.

Across Turkey, approximately 41.5 million of the 48.8 million registered voters went to the polls and cast a valid vote.

---

## Towards the Presidential Election

![](/media/weebly/7137999-75a815ee0c.jpg)

The first person to announce his candidacy as the presidential election approached was Prime Minister Recep Tayyip Erdoğan. Afterwards, the CHP and MHP decided to join forces in the election and put forward a joint candidate. Ekmeleddin İhsanoğlu, the two opposition parties' joint candidate, faced considerable controversy because he was not widely known, and went through a difficult introduction campaign. Finally, Selahattin Demirtaş announced his candidacy with support from the BDP and HDP constituencies.

Before the election, my personal view was that Erdoğan would finish ahead, but that the election would go to a second round. I expected Erdoğan to reach the presidential palace with around 51–52 percent after a closely fought second round. However, the first-round results showed that a lot of water had passed under the bridge over the past six months.

---

## The Election of Whose Voters Turned Out More

According to the unofficial results of yesterday's presidential election, approximately:

- Recep Tayyip Erdoğan: 20.5 million
- Ekmeleddin İhsanoğlu: 15.2 million
- Selahattin Demirtaş: 3.8 million

votes were cast.

Approximately 39 million of the 55 million registered voters participated in the election by casting a valid vote.

![Erdoğan still could not reach the warm seas](/media/weebly/1407700685-e2fd133147.png)

![](/media/weebly/1407700733-8d3bede1f3.png)

According to these unofficial results, Tayyip Erdoğan not only maintained his own voter base in the presidential election but also managed to draw conservative opposition votes to his side. I think the nearly one million voters held by the Saadet Party played an important role in increasing Erdoğan's vote. As for the other winner of the election in my view, I see that Selahattin Demirtaş increased the HDP and BDP bloc's vote by more than one million, bringing it close to four million. Demirtaş, who showed that he could challenge the ten-percent threshold in a general election if the presidential system is not introduced, is probably pleased with this result.

Now let us turn to the election's loser, Ekmeleddin İhsanoğlu. Not only did İhsanoğlu fail to gain votes for the CHP and MHP camp; he also lost them a considerable number. I think this was because he was not sufficiently well known by the public. İhsanoğlu, who announced his candidacy one month before the election, apparently could not introduce himself to voters adequately in that time. Add to this the fact that some of the CHP's liberal voters and the MHP's conservative voters were not particularly enthusiastic about the CHP–MHP partnership, and İhsanoğlu's vote fell well below the combined number received by the two opposition parties in the 30 March elections.

So what happened to the CHP/MHP votes that did not go to İhsanoğlu? I think some of the votes coming from the MHP, particularly those of conservative voters, shifted to Tayyip Erdoğan. Nor should we overlook the possibility that some liberal-minded voters from the CHP and other left-wing parties voted for Selahattin Demirtaş. However, the results suggest that the biggest factor behind this outcome was the number of voters who did not go to the polls. Compared with the local elections on 30 March, 2.5 million voters chose not to vote. In an election this critical for the opposition, losing two and a half million votes for nothing should be considered a serious figure. Add the votes lost to the other candidates, and defeat became inevitable for İhsanoğlu.

> The presidential election result figures will be updated when the YSK announces the official results.

---

## Final Word

![](/media/weebly/1407700862-cf82c08460.png)

As I finish my analysis, I would like to share my own final thoughts.

I had regarded Erdoğan becoming president as a certainty since the local elections on 30 March, but his being elected so easily in the first round was a surprise to me. A new Turkey shaped by Tayyip Erdoğan now awaits us. Frankly, I am not optimistic about this new Turkey at all. I think we will frequently encounter restrictions on people's freedoms in the coming period. I also expect a ruling party that interferes more in our private lives. I hope I turn out to be wrong and that these things do not happen in the future.

As for my views on the opposition parties, I think the joint umbrella-candidate strategy pursued by the CHP and MHP ended in a complete fiasco. This also shows us that the frequently discussed idea of opposition parties joining forces against the government in a general election is very utopian. I now think that the chances of such a union ending in victory are rather low.

The opposition parties, which have lost two elections in six months and, in one way or another, let a considerable portion of their voters slip away, must certainly make radical changes. I believe that the leaders of the CHP and MHP, who failed to make their influence felt in the latest elections and caused their parties to lose votes through poor strategies, should resign.

Wishing for a happier and freer Turkey...
