Traffic rules, which are the same everywhere else in the world, work a little differently in Istanbul. Here are some golden rules you need to know before venturing into Istanbul traffic.

![](/media/weebly/1384001421-e19f76f612.png)

They could not agree on who had the right of way.

**1-In Istanbul, whoever has the bigger vehicle has the right of way.**

It does not matter what road you are on or how fast you are driving. You must give way to vehicles bigger than yours at junctions and connecting roads. Otherwise, you may end up in an accident. On top of that, you could be seriously injured in the ensuing argument about your failure to give way.

Naturally, this makes the IETT buses, which are always on the road, the sole rulers of the streets.

![](/media/weebly/1384001451-2d235225a4.png)

An ordinary day in Istanbul traffic.

**2-You must be ready for a fight at all times.**

In Istanbul traffic, there is always a chance of getting into an argument with the driver next to you. Many drivers here feel as if they are in a race. As they try to reach their destination as quickly as possible, they will see you as a rival and start an argument over the slightest mistake.

![](/media/weebly/1384001553-d6c32d6229.png)

The governor is waiting for us at the bridge exit, chief.

**3-If you install flashing lights on your vehicle, you can use the emergency lane.**

Fed up with Istanbul's endless traffic? All you need to do is fit your vehicle with a flashing light. After that, you can use all of the emergency lanes. Your risk of getting caught is also very low, because no police officer will risk accidentally pulling over a governor's or mayor's vehicle to check you.

![](/media/weebly/1384001562-8817ae0d4e.png)

A representative image of the miraculous button.

**4-If you turn on your hazard lights, you can leave your car wherever you want.**

Couldn't find somewhere to park, and need to pop out somewhere? With the help of the little red button in your car with the double triangle on it, you can turn on your hazard lights and abandon your car wherever you want. Of course, while you are trying to buy a packet of cigarettes, some inconsiderate drivers behind you may honk their horns. Pretend you cannot hear them.

![](/media/weebly/1384001580-dfd268ecbf.png)

Keep flowing, brother, keep flowing.

**5-Here, lanes are marked only for four-wheeled vehicles.**

While you are sitting in your car in traffic, a motorcycle may suddenly fly past you at jet speed. Do not be alarmed. In fact, in particularly congested areas you may even encounter motorcycles riding on the pavement. So here is an important reminder: when walking along the pavement as a pedestrian in a heavily congested area, keep one ear listening behind you, or you may suddenly find yourself at the mercy of a motorcycle.

![](/media/weebly/1384001598-b025fd291a.png)

The Turk is ahead, the Turk! Onwards!

**6-If the light is green, move forward even if your way is blocked.**

This is a common occurrence in Istanbul traffic. City buses and yellow taxis are particularly keen to put this rule into practice. Even if traffic has stopped ten metres ahead, you move forward and wait in the middle of the junction. After all, your light is green, and waiting beside a traffic light while it is green is simply out of the question.

![](/media/weebly/1384001636-182ff91111.png)

**7-In Istanbul, men are better drivers.**

In Istanbul, male drivers always have the upper hand over female drivers. The testosterone hormone secreted by the male reproductive organ is thought to improve a person's driving abilities. That is why, whenever a vehicle makes a mistake, the first thing people check is the driver's gender.

This is also why women who drive lorries, buses or taxis make the evening news.

> Although this example is not from Istanbul, similar situations occur frequently.

**8-In Istanbul traffic, using substances before driving is normal.**

In Istanbul, you need to be able to drive not only when sober but also when drunk. A driver who cannot drive after drinking is treated as a novice. Using drugs is also normal in Istanbul traffic. Here is an example from the news:

Traffic teams tested 73 drivers for drugs during inspections over the past two months. In these checks, drugs were detected in three out of 16 commercial taxi drivers, one lorry and van driver, 15 out of 49 car drivers, and six out of seven minibus and bus drivers.
