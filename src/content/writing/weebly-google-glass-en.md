*Last Saturday, an event called DEVFEST took place at ITU's Süleyman Demirel Cultural Centre. Mobile application development and careers in the mobile software industry were prominent topics. However, there were sessions on almost every subject related to the software world. ITU IEEE deserves a big round of applause for successfully organising such a large-scale event. In this post, I am looking at Glass, one of Google's projects that has attracted attention recently.*

![](/media/weebly/1383491786-c3c805807a.png)

The glasses seem to have made me lose myself.

The most eye-catching product at the event was Google Glass. Although Glass, one of Google's important projects, is still under development, it has already begun to be tested around the world. I had the opportunity to try it myself at DEVFEST Istanbul.

First of all, when we pick up the glasses, we encounter a very light and comfortable design. Everyone who tried the glasses at the event was happy with them and had no problems with comfort.

To give a general overview of the glasses' technical specifications:

- One camera capable of recording at 720p
- A screen with a resolution of 640x320
- A total of 16 GB of memory, of which 12 GB is usable
- Audio playback
- Voice and multi-touch pad controls
- Wi-Fi and Bluetooth

![](/media/weebly/1383497196-9388c1c120.jpg)

![](/media/weebly/1383492184-58959878b8.jpg)

At the moment, the commands the glasses can carry out are limited, but they are ready for most situations that might be needed in everyday use. In general, they have the basic features of any mobile device. They can take pictures, record video, give directions, send messages and make video calls over the internet, and search the internet through Google... I hope that these commands will increase in the future.

During the presentation, we were shown some example videos from everyday use. There was also a live video call over the internet. Although the 3G connection caused some image distortion during the call, videos recorded previously showed us that the camera could record video at a very good quality. As for controls, the device is primarily intended to be used by voice through Google Talk, but it can also be controlled using the touch surface on its right-hand side. It is really nice that it recognises almost all of the voice commands. You do not have any difficulties using it. As for the screen, its position and size are really suitable for use, and it does not bother your eyes much when you are not using it. In any case, after a while without interacting with the device, the screen locks itself and turns off its light, as on a phone.

Beyond these features, developers are continuing to work on different ideas. One application I came across online, which really interested me, lets you use the glasses as a guide while repairing a car. It demonstrated that the glasses could be used for a much wider variety of tasks in the future.

![](/media/weebly/1383498788-9be86a004a.jpg)

![](/media/weebly/1383494939-f12bb5add1.jpg)

Wear caused by heat.

Of course, as with any new technology, there are areas that need improvement. One of the most important is that the device gets hot while recording long videos, even causing redness on the skin. The heat can also damage the device itself. For now, Google apparently repairs devices damaged by heat free of charge.

The device also has some battery-related problems. It can record only 30–35 minutes at 720p using its built-in battery, while its average battery life outside video recording is four to five hours. The application developer who gave us the presentation explained that he used a portable battery to extend its operating time when using Glass for long periods.

There are also problems concerning privacy. There is a risk of recording people around you in public without permission, or accidentally recording unwanted footage of yourself and those around you by forgetting to turn the camera off. In my view, however, these risks are not as important as the technical problems, because they can also occur with any mobile device that has a camera.

![](/media/weebly/1383498893-3bb6e5caec.png)

Google Glass is currently available only to developers in America. It is not possible to give a firm release date, but it is expected to be released in the second half of 2014. I think that sales in Turkey will probably not begin until 2015.

There is no definite information about the price either, but some statements originating from Google say that the device will not be more expensive than an average smartphone. My own guess is that it will go on sale in Turkey for around 1,500 lira, though of course it is not possible to say anything for certain right now.

Finally, I want to emphasise this point: Google Glass is not an alternative to smartphones or tablet computers. It is a device in an entirely new category, independent of them.

To cut a long story short, I saw it, tried it and liked it. There are a few problems, but I do not think they are anything Google cannot overcome. If I had to give it a score, it definitely deserves eight out of ten.
