Computer Virus Hoaxes

5/4/2017

Comments

 
In advanced hacking scenarios, attackers generally try to exploit the target machine’s security flaws. On the other hand, influencing the user by carefully designed social engineering technics can be very effective to create vulnerabilities, or even perform the attack itself. In this blog, I will examine an example of honor system virus, Amish virus. Also, I will mention virus hoaxes briefly .

What is Amish Virus

Amish virus is a virus hoax [1]. It is classified as honor system virus [2]. In honor system virus’ attacking attempt, the email claims to be written by the Amish or other similar low-technology populations who have no computers, programming skills or electricity to create viruses and thus ask you to delete your own hard drive contents manually after forwarding the message to your friends. [1]

Virus Hoaxes

A typical hoax email contains three essential parts. The hook, the threat, and the request. The main goal of the hoaxes to spread as much as possible on the internet, and influence the utmost people. A virus hoax does not perform malicious activity itself, but wastes resources, both human and computer. Furthermore, they sometimes try to create vulnerabilities or disorders on the recipient’s system by commanding some orders to perform. The situation can be explained by frequently mentioned psychological conformity.
• Hooks may point source of information as widely known trusted organizations or companies, so that, recipients trust take Sirius the email.
• The hoax's threat aims to confuse, and frighten recipient with technical sounding details. So, the recipient moves away from critical thinking.
• A recipient may want to feel important, may want to ingratiate themselves to other users, or may genuinely want to warn others.

The hoax commanded Windows users to find programs with bear-like icons and makes them delete the associated program. As it turns out, the program was Java debugger utility which was a legitimate program.

The virus is not detected by McAfee or Norton and it stays quiet for 14 days before damaging the system. The virus can be cleaned before it deletes the files from your system. Go to Start, click "Search" 2.- In the "Files or Folders option" write the name jdbgmgr.exe 3.- Be sure that you are searching in the drive "C" 4.- Click "find now" 5.- If the virus is there (it has a little bear-like icon with the name of jdbgmgr.exe DO NOT OPEN IT FOR ANY REASON 6.- Right click and delete it (it will go to the Recycle bin) 7.- Go to the recycle bin and delete it or empty the recycle bin. Are Virus Hoaxes and Amish Virus Real Threat? [4][6]

After my research, I think Amish viruses and hoaxes are true viruses, but they do not directly aim to perform a malicious activity on the recipient’s system. However, they harm people and systems in other ways such as giving orders to recipients to delete some important files that will affect the availability and integrity of the system. For those who can differentiate a hoax mail from a real one, it is a nuisance to read and remove the repeated warnings. For others, it causes pointless stress and decreases productivity. Furthermore, worried system admins and companies may try to take unnecessary prevention measures and spent resources on that cause. We can say that hoax emails are a kind of spams, and spams are a troubling concept for internet services. Some estimates claim one-third of the emails sent is spam. ISP and mail server providers need to take steps to handle this redundant stress on the bandwidth, and storage. Spams also affect recipients by wasting their time and increasing the ISP bills.

Prevention [4]

The trend of virus hoaxes appears to be on the decline, possibly, because of the effective spam filtering of current service providers. Even without technical solutions, training and creating awareness is very effective.
